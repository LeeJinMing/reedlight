# Reedlight

**A sideways step brings three paper cuts into register. Hold that viewpoint to pull a kingfisher print.**

## Who it is for

Reedlight is a small printmaking exhibit for gallery visitors. A label can tell you that nearby objects appear to move faster than distant ones. Here you can lean a few centimetres, watch a feather slide past a patch of orange, and discover that difference for yourself.

## The experience

Three transparent sheets hang at different depths. Begin with **Start**, then move sideways with the paper in view. The scattered cuts line up around a cream eye. For a moment the orange breast, long bill and blue-green wing belong to one bird. Hold still and **Print** becomes available. Selecting it pulls a flat impression of the bird. Use **Replay** and return to the starting point to separate the pieces again.

The visitor can point their head at a control for one second to select it. This leaves both hands free while they search. Directional cues help visitors who pass the registration point or change height or distance.

## How it works

The three sheets sit 76, 101 and 130 centimetres from the starting frame. Each carries a different set of cuts from an original illustration. Their size and position share a single projection from a viewpoint 16 centimetres to the right. The camera moves while all three sheets stay fixed. The controller compares the apparent centres and scale of the near and far sheets, then checks a short dwell before allowing the print. The flat impression appears only when the visitor chooses Print.

During the first editor walkthrough, the aligned bird lost its tail at the edge of the view. We reframed all three layers around a common centre and replaced the hard-to-read instructions with illustrated cue cards. The bird remains fully visible before Print in the recorded walkthrough.

## Tools, artwork and AI

The entrant directed the moment of discovery. We planned Reedlight with the Devpost Learn skill pack and built the SPECS scene with Snap CLAD, Lens Studio 5.25.0 and the Lens Studio MCP. OpenAI Codex assisted with design, code, original geometric illustration, cue cards, copy, tests and video captions. Python and Pillow draw the art from source. The Lens uses Snap’s SPECS Base Template, InteractionKit and UIKit. The demo is a silent recording of the actual Lens Studio Interactive Preview with English captions. Verification covers the desktop preview. Physical SPECS comfort and hand tracking remain untested.
